Honeybees communicate the location of food through an elaborate dance. When a scout bee returns to the hive after finding flowers, it performs a series of movements on the vertical surface of the honeycomb. The direction of the dance indicates the angle of the food relative to the sun, while the duration of each waggle signals the distance. Remarkably, the other bees can read this information in complete darkness, sensing the vibrations and movements of the dancer. The more excited the dance, the richer the food source it describes. This system allows a single scout to recruit hundreds of workers to a promising field within minutes. For a long time, scientists doubted that such a tiny brain could convey such precise information, but careful experiments have confirmed that the dance is a genuine language of movement.
