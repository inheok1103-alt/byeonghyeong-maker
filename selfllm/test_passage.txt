Many people believe that talent alone determines success. However, research on expert performers tells a different story. When psychologists studied musicians who had reached the highest levels, they found that the amount of deliberate practice, not natural gift, separated the best from the rest. The students who practiced with clear goals and immediate feedback improved far more quickly than those who simply repeated familiar pieces. Deliberate practice is demanding because it forces learners to work at the edge of their current abilities. Such training often feels uncomfortable, and this discomfort is precisely the signal that learning is taking place. In other words, the path to excellence is built not on what we are born with but on how we choose to train.
