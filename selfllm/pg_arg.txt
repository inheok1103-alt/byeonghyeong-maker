Many parents believe that praising their children's intelligence will build confidence. However, research suggests the opposite may be true. When children are told they are smart, they come to see ability as a fixed trait. As a result, they avoid difficult tasks that might reveal their limits, and they give up quickly when they struggle. In contrast, children who are praised for their effort learn to view challenges as opportunities to grow. Therefore, parents should focus their praise on the process rather than the person. Instead of saying "You are so clever," they should say "You worked really hard on that." In this way, children develop the resilience they need to face difficulties. What we praise shapes how our children understand success itself.
