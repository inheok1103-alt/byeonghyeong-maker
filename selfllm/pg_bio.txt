Rachel Carson was born in 1907 in Springdale, Pennsylvania. As a child, she loved exploring the woods and streams near her family farm. She first studied English at college, but a required biology course changed the direction of her life, and she switched her major to zoology. After earning a master's degree in 1932, she worked as a writer for the U.S. Bureau of Fisheries. In 1962, she published Silent Spring, a book that warned about the dangers of chemical pesticides. Although the chemical industry attacked her work fiercely, the book led to a nationwide ban on DDT and helped launch the modern environmental movement. Carson died of cancer in 1964, two years after her most famous book appeared. She never married, and she is remembered today as one of the founders of environmental science.
